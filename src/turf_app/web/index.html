                    How this report was made
                    1. The brand's results were matched to US metro areas ().
                    2. Its best markets were picked by , among metros with at least 1% of spend.
                    3. Every metro was described by its mix of local businesses (Overture Maps) and population (US Census). The kinds of business used for are listed above.
                    4. Turf checked whether those patterns predict the brand's results in markets they weren't fitted on, against 999 shuffled versions of the same data.
                    5. The recommendations are the metros that look most like the best markets, where the brand spends little or nothing. Expected results come from the brand's most similar existing markets.

                    About this demo

                    Turf is a demo built for a take-home assignment, not a production service.

                    • Sample brands are fictional. Their results are synthetic, generated to show how the report works. They aren't real companies or real performance.
                    • No real ad accounts. A production version would connect to a brand's ad accounts. Here you can upload a CSV (read in your browser, used for one report, and never stored), or connect a simulated ad account: a stand-in platform inside this demo that Turf syncs the way it would a real one. Its synced data is synthetic and deleted within a day.
                    • No real-time AI generation. To keep the demo free to run, the creative briefs were written ahead of time for the sample brands' recommendations. Everything else in the report is calculated from data on request.
                    • The data is real and public: Overture Maps places (release …) and US Census metro areas and population.
                    • Recommendations are a starting point. They come from patterns in local businesses, which suggest where an audience lives but don't measure demand. Test before moving budget.
